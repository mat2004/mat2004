# Social life: getting out more

## The honest truth
- **Confidence comes after action, not before.** You won't feel ready, so you go anyway, and it gets easier each time.
- Most people are nervous too, and grateful when someone else starts the conversation.
- Friendships mostly come from **seeing the same people again and again** (class, club, team, work). That's why joining something that meets every week matters more than going to one big party.

## The ladder (it's in the app's Social tab)
1. **Warm-up:** eye contact and a smile, chat with cashiers, reply to group chats.
2. **Small talk:** questions to classmates, genuine compliments, asking coworkers about their weekend.
3. **Initiate:** suggest coffee, message an old friend, go to one event.
4. **Show up:** join something that repeats weekly, stay 45 minutes at events, organise something small.
5. **Build:** see the same people again, introduce yourself to strangers, go on solo outings.

Clear 2 challenges on a level before moving up. Repeating a level is fine.

## Conversation basics
- **Openers about the shared situation** work best: "Did you get what he meant about the assignment?", "Is this the queue for…?", "How long have you been coming here?"
- **Ask, then follow up.** "How was your weekend?" → "Oh nice, where did you go?" A follow-up question shows you're actually listening.
- **Remember one detail** and bring it up next time. People love it.
- **Exit cleanly:** "Good talking to you, see you Thursday." Done.
- **Invite low-pressure:** "A few of us are getting coffee after class, want to come?" It's easy to say yes to and easy to say no to.

## Where to meet people (as a working student)
- Uni clubs and societies, especially ones with a weekly activity (sport, hiking, board games, languages, volunteering).
- Study groups. Start one if nobody else has.
- Gym classes, climbing gyms, running clubs. Same faces every week.
- Coworkers: lunch, after-work drinks, saying yes when invited.
- Old friends: one message is enough. "Hey, been ages, want to grab a coffee this week?"

## The 30-day yes rule
For the next 30 days, say yes to every reasonable invite. Leave after an hour if you want to, but go.

## When it feels bad
Awkward moments are normal and nobody remembers them except you. If anxiety is strong or constant, talking to your uni's counselling service is a normal and smart thing to do. It's usually free for students.
